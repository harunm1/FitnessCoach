# Li et al. (2026), MoveBench: a rubric-based benchmark for LLMs in sports and health coaching

**Citation.** Li, Y., Bian, J., Wang, J., Ding, P., and Gu, C. (2026). MoveBench: A rubric-based benchmark for evaluating large language models in sports and health coaching. arXiv:2610.26185v2 (preprint, 26 Oct 2026; authors at Keep, a Chinese fitness platform). Data: https://github.com/Keep-Tech/MoveBench
**Read:** all 14 pages (main text, references, appendix A with three worked items and a full rubric), 8 Nov 2026.
**Feeds:** #64 and #65 (coach evaluation), `docs/COACH_EVAL.md`, final report evaluation section.

## What they built

- 809 items, 5,012 rubric criteria: 310 multiple choice (113 single-select, 197 multi-select) and 499 open questions; by capability 257 plan generation (PG), 291 sport-log analysis (DA), 261 knowledge QA.
- Items generated by Gemini 3.0 Pro (temperature 0.7) from user profiles sampled to match their platform's real distribution (BMI, age, sex, training habits); DA items embed real activity logs (pace, heart rate, cadence, RPE). LLM quality screen rejects moderate or high risk items, then domain experts approve, revise or reject.
- **PG rubric, eight dimensions:** medical safety boundaries; structured metadata (duration, difficulty, equipment); description quality; readability; program-level adaptation to goal and level; environmental adaptation (space, time, social constraints); program structure (sequencing, rest, workload balance, progression); action-level adaptation (each exercise safe, executable, not redundant, fits the user).
- DA rubric (six) and QA rubric (four) add data-processing accuracy, risk identification, scientific accuracy, teaching quality and boundary keeping.
- **Scoring:** multiple choice is exact match, no partial credit. Open answers are scored per criterion by three judges (Gemini 3.1 Pro 0.35, Claude 4.6 Sonnet 0.35, GPT-5.2 0.30). Safety is a separate vote: medical overreach, unsafe guidance, or physiological hallucination is a redline; **two of three redline votes set the item to zero**; one vote is kept for audit only. Unscored items (API failures) are reported as coverage, never silently dropped.
- The worked open item shows the rubric style: mostly binary criteria (goal consistency, exercise validity, structure 1–2 / 4–7 / 1–2 minutes, environment fit, every stated constraint honoured, stop conditions present), plus counted completeness items.

## Results

- 15 models; overall 0.875 (GPT-6) to 0.943 (Claude 4.6 Opus).
- Single-select: every model 1.000 (ceiling, no discrimination). Multi-select 0.888–0.980. Open questions 0.812–0.917, the widest spread. A model can top multiple-choice and trail on open questions (Gemini 4 Argon: MS 0.980, OQ 0.812).
- Domain unevenness: e.g. GLM-5.3 QA 0.955 vs PG 0.873; Qwen3.8-Max QA 0.936 vs PG 0.881. DA scores were comparatively high; PG and QA were more uneven. The authors call this the gap between knowing and doing.
- **Judge reliability (30 open items, 300 criteria, three experts plus a senior adjudicator):** experts agreed with the adjudicated reference 80.6–86.0% (tolerance 0.2); judges only 62.3–76.3% (GPT 62.3%, Claude 64.3% with 20 criteria unclassified, Gemini 76.3%). Across three repeated runs the judges' scores moved: SD 0.047 (Claude) to 0.107 (GPT), ranges 0.09 to 0.21.
- Their own conclusion: a structured basis for evaluation, not certification that coaching is safe or effective.

## Limits they state

Judge-to-expert alignment is moderate; multiple choice is saturated; three runs is too few to fix rankings; items are LLM-generated (then expert-checked); one platform's user distribution; the paper does not report how often the safety override fired.

## What it means for FitnessCoach

1. Our plan checks in `verify` already cover three of their eight PG dimensions in code: medical boundaries (guardrails), environmental adaptation (equipment), action-level adaptation (exercise fits the user). Add the missing ones we can check deterministically: structure (warm-up, main, cool-down present; rest days) and metadata completeness.
2. A redline is a hard zero, separate from quality points. Our guardrails do the same in code; keep safety failures reported separately from quality scores in `COACH_EVAL.md`.
3. Do not gate CI on an LLM judge: agreement with experts was 62–76% and scores drifted across runs. Keep CI rule-based; if a judge is used for the report, show human spot checks beside it and report run-to-run spread.
4. Report per profile and per dimension (#64), plus coverage (how many runs failed or fell back), never one average.
5. Binary, constraint-by-constraint criteria (did it honour the 6–10 minute window, the small space, the equipment) are easy to turn into unit tests on our `evalProfiles`.
