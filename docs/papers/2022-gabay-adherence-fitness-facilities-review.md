# Gabay & Oravițan (2022): factors affecting adherence to physical activity in fitness facilities

## Citation

Gabay, M., & Oravițan, M. (2022). The factors affecting adherence to physical activity in fitness facility settings: a narrative review. _Timișoara Physical Education and Rehabilitation Journal_, 15(29), 46–61. https://doi.org/10.2478/tperj-2022-0013

## Read

All 16 pages (pp. 46–61), including Table 1 and the reference list. 8 November 2026.

## Feeds

Proposal (motivation, adherence problem), #48 guardrails and tone, #63 and #70 chat, plan generation (frequency defaults).

## What they did

Narrative review using PRISMA reporting. Four databases (EBSCO, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science), fitness-facility terms combined with adherence or dropout terms, articles online before 31 January 2022. Inclusion: English, adults, generally healthy, both sexes, set in or recruiting from a fitness facility, peer reviewed. 365 records, 283 after deduplication, 32 full texts, 19 included. Sixteen quantitative, three qualitative; 13 used a theory (nine self-determination theory). Pooled sample 9,358 (58% women), study sizes 12 to 5,248. Findings grouped into social context, habit and past behaviour, professional support, motives and barriers, personal characteristics, and adherence rates. No meta-analysis; no risk-of-bias assessment.

## Results

- Dropout is high: about half of new members cut back after the first month; 40 to 65% of new exercisers are predicted to drop out in three to six months; only 37% reported regular attendance in the first year (Gjestvang 2020).
- Sperandei et al. (2016), n = 5,240, unsupervised gym: more than half did not complete three months; fewer than 5% stayed twelve months; 3.7% expected beyond a year. The review states the 12-month figure two ways ("fewer than 5%" on p. 54, "5%" on p. 55); cite Sperandei directly, not the review, for the number.
- Strongest predictor of regular attendance in the first year: enjoyment, then self-efficacy, then social support from friends and family (Gjestvang 2021).
- Past behaviour is the strongest predictor of future attendance and offsets the effect of intention (Rodrigues 2020, 2021).
- Habit: at least four sessions a week for about six weeks to form a habit (Kaushal & Rhodes 2015); at least two a week consistently to maintain (Rodrigues 2021). The authors combine these into their own "2–4 sessions a week for six weeks" suggestion; that range is the review's synthesis, not a tested finding.
- Consistency (same time and place) was the most important predictor of habit change; new exercisers need a fixed schedule (Kaushal & Rhodes 2015; Tappe 2013).
- Personal training or supervision linked with more autonomous motivation and better adherence (Klain 2015); unsupervised adherence is very low (Sperandei 2016).
- Highest dropout risk: younger people with high BMI driven by weight loss; younger, less experienced women report weaker intentions (Sperandei 2016; Rodrigues 2019).
- External motivation dominates early; internal motivation carries long-term maintenance (Ortís 2007). A self-efficacy intervention helped only in the short term (Middelkamp 2017).
- Most common barrier: lack of time (Gjestvang 2020).
- Instructors can support or thwart basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness); the review calls social context a double-edged sword.

## Limits

Narrative review, no pooled estimate, no quality appraisal of included studies. Mostly self-report questionnaires. Gym and fitness-club settings only; adults only; one search with a fixed cut-off date. Some findings are stated slightly differently between Results and Discussion (the Sperandei 12-month figure; "psychological" versus physiological motives on p. 54 against Table 1).

## What it means for FitnessCoach

- Proposal motivation: cite Sperandei (2016) for the dropout figure and this review for the factor list; do not quote "2–4 a week" as evidence-based without saying it is the review's synthesis.
- Plan defaults: a fixed schedule (same days each week) for beginners matches the consistency finding; the weekly planner should keep days stable across regenerations rather than reshuffling.
- Coach tone (#63, #70): support autonomy and competence, avoid controlling language; enjoyment and self-efficacy are the levers the literature names. This matches Bloom's autonomy-supportive framing.
- Guardrails (#48): weight-loss-driven beginners are the highest dropout group; coach replies to weight-loss goals should not promise outcomes and should emphasise consistency over intensity.
- Evaluation: adherence is the outcome the literature cares about; any user study we run can measure sessions logged per week against the plan, which the Workout table already records.
