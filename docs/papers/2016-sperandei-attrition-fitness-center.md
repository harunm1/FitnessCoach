# Sperandei, Vieira & Reis (2016): attrition among fitness centre members

## Citation

Sperandei, S., Vieira, M. C., & Reis, A. C. (2016). Adherence to physical activity in an unsupervised setting: Explanatory variables for high attrition rates among fitness center members. _Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport_, 19(11), 916–920. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2015.12.522

## Read

Full text, all sections, Table 1 and references (journal HTML), 8 November 2026.

## Feeds

Proposal Section 1 (motivation); adherence measures for any user study.

## What they did

Retrospective cohort from payment records of one mid-sized fitness centre in central Rio de Janeiro: 5,240 first-time members who joined between January 2005 and June 2014, followed for 12 months or until a month without payment ("abandonment"). Kaplan-Meier survival, Harrington-Fleming tests to screen variables, then a Cox proportional hazards model weighted for month and year of entry.

## Results

- Probability of still being a member after one month 0.76, after two months 0.54; median membership three months; 3.7% stayed more than 12 consecutive months. The abstract states this as 63% abandoning before the third month and under 4% past 12 months.
- Higher risk of leaving (hazard ratios): age up to 25 (1.27) and 26 to 35 (1.09) against over 35; never active before (1.55) and inactive (1.20) against active; BMI above 25 (1.08); weight-loss motive (1.09). Members who did not cite hypertrophy (1.10), health (1.07) or aesthetics (1.06) left sooner.
- Best profile (over 35, already active, not motivated by weight loss, motivated by hypertrophy, health and aesthetics) still had only about a 30% chance of passing six months and about 10% of passing twelve.
- Only 16% of leavers came back within 30 days and another 16% within 12 months.

## Limits

One centre; adherence is payment, not attendance; leaving the gym is not proof of stopping exercise; no data on visit frequency or reasons for leaving.

## What it means for FitnessCoach

- The proposal's "63% of new members discontinued their memberships before the third month" matches the abstract; "stopped paying before their third month" is the precise reading.
- Our users are the high-risk group (beginners, often weight-loss goals), so the first eight weeks are where the coach has to work: a fixed weekly schedule, early wins on strength rather than scale weight, and no promises about weight loss.
- If we run a pilot, measure adherence as logged sessions per planned session over time (the Workout table already records it), not sign-ups.
