# Sperandei, Vieira & Reis (2016): attrition among fitness centre members

## Citation

Sperandei, S., Vieira, M. C., & Reis, A. C. (2016). Adherence to physical activity in an unsupervised setting: Explanatory variables for high attrition rates among fitness center members. _Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport_, 19(11), 916–920. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2015.12.522 (PMID 26874647)

## Read

**Abstract only** (PubMed record, 8 November 2026). The full text is not open access and was not read. Everything below comes from the abstract or from Gabay & Oravițan (2022), which summarises it; treat it as provisional until the full text is read.

## Feeds

Proposal Section 1 (motivation).

## What they did

Retrospective longitudinal study of 5,240 members of one fitness centre in Rio de Janeiro who joined between January 2005 and June 2014, followed for 12 months or until they cancelled. Cox proportional hazards regression on the risk of abandonment.

## Results (abstract)

- The survival curve shows 63% of new members abandon before the third month, and fewer than 4% stay more than 12 months of continuous activity.
- Age, previous activity level, starting BMI and motives (weight loss, hypertrophy, health, aesthetics) are associated with abandonment risk.
- Even members with the most favourable combination of variables still had a high risk of leaving before 12 months.

## Limits

One centre, one city, unsupervised setting; membership records only, so leaving this gym is counted as abandonment even if the person trains elsewhere (Middelkamp et al. 2017, via Gabay, found 30% of leavers still trained at another club).

## What it means for FitnessCoach

- Proposal Section 1 states "63% of new members discontinued their memberships before the third month". The abstract supports 63% but phrases it as abandoning activities, estimated from a survival curve. The proposal wording is close enough; a stricter version is "63% of new members stopped attending before their third month".
- Gabay & Oravițan (2022) report this study as "more than 50%" and "fewer than 5%"; cite Sperandei directly for numbers.
