# Design review: the coach, 3 November 2026

A ten-minute review of the coach after a week of building, taken against the course presentation rubric (6 minutes plus 2 minutes of Q&A; core requirements shown working locally or deployed; at least two advanced features demonstrated, or explained with a diagram and a completion plan) and the 23 November demo freeze.

## Where the coach stands

- On `main`: plan generation from the exercise library, the verifier, one regeneration then the rule-based plan, and the Groq-then-Gemini provider chain with retries (#30).
- In review: the eval and its CI replay (#32, #55), running and hybrid weeks and saving them (#44, #45, #47, #54), guardrails (#48), per-call logging (#49), the chat turn (#53) and streaming (#56).
- Not yet in the app: onboarding builds the rule-based plan only, and there is no chat route or screen, so no user reaches the model yet.

## Risks found

1. **Ten pull requests in review, stacked three or four deep, the week the proposal is due.** The bottleneck is coordination, not code.
2. **Nobody owns the demo environment.**
3. **Free-tier limits.** Four people hitting Groq (about one full plan a minute) and Gemini Flash (20 requests a day) at once on demo day would exhaust both.
4. **Over-triggering guardrails.** "How do I avoid knee pain when squatting?" gets the same stop reply as a pain report.
5. **Streaming** is the most fragile piece and is not on the core path.

## Decisions

1. **Onboarding shows the rule-based plan instantly.** No model call at sign-up: no 2 to 15 second wait and no quota spent. A "Personalize with the coach" button calls the model on demand, and its plan goes through the same verifier.
2. **The demo shows three coach moments:** an AI-generated plan, a quick chat reply, and a graceful fallback when the provider drops. The fallback must be immediate in the demo, so demo-critical coach outputs are pre-generated or cached.
3. **A question about pain is not a pain report.** A report still stops the exercise. A question gets general, safe programming guidance (warm-up, range of motion, load) with a line that it is not medical advice, and no diagnosis.
4. **Streaming is the first thing cut** if the core path slips.
5. **Merge order: schema first, then the logic built on it, then the screens.** Review requests give three days; an author merges after 72 hours without a review; no teammate gets more than three small pull requests a day, spaced 1.5 to 2 days apart.
6. **A deployed preview with a free hosted PostgreSQL by Sun 15 Nov.** The course accepts a local demo, so this is for testing the integration and for the pilot, not a grading gate.
7. **The body-map follow-up asks three or four bounded questions** (where it is felt, at which point in the rep, the weight against usual) before the coach answers, and sits in the coach's domain logic.

## Pilot, from Sun 15 Nov

Kept small so it adds no work for the team: the coach owner and local test runs against the real database, before teammates use it. Measured: response time, fallback rate (from the per-call log) and whether a logged workout completes. Problems go on GitHub issues labelled `pilot`.

**Demo ready means:** onboarding shows a plan with no wait, the coach's plan and chat work, and a provider failure falls back without an error screen.

## Open questions for later

- How schema migrations are handled once real workouts are saved (no destructive migrations after the pilot starts; backfill instead of drop).
- How to watch API usage before we hit a limit (the per-call log, #52, and the providers' rate-limit headers).
